Friedlingstein, Pierre, et al. "Global Carbon Budget 2024." Earth System Science Data, Global Carbon Project, 2024, doi.org/10.18160/gcp-2024.

The Global Carbon Budget is an annual report coordinated by the Global Carbon Project, made up of over a hundred researchers from institutions across the world. The 2024 edition, led by Pierre Friedlingstein of the University of Exeter, quantifies CO₂ emissions from fossil fuels, land-use change, and natural sinks that estimate total human-caused emissions at roughly 37.4 gigatonnes for the year. It's regarded as the most comprehensive accounting of the global carbon cycle published annually, and is presented at the United Nations COP climate conferences each year. Its finding that emissions remain at record highs despite decades of international climate agreements is the central tension the project is built around.

Lan, Xin, Pieter Tans, and K.W. Thoning. "Trends in Globally-Averaged CO₂ Determined from NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory Measurements." NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory, 2024, doi.org/10.15138/9N0H-ZH07.

The NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory has maintained continuous atmospheric CO₂ measurements since the late 1950s, starting at Mauna Loa, Hawaii, and expanding to over fifty sampling sites. As one of the longest-running scientific monitoring programs on the planet, the NOAA CO₂ record is among the most cited datasets in all of climate science. The institutional weight and duration of this data was important to me in making the project's numbers feel undeniable rather than abstract.

World Meteorological Organization. State of the Global Climate 2023. WMO-No. 1347, WMO, 2024, wmo.int/publication-series/state-of-global-climate.

The World Meteorological Organization is a specialized agency of the United Nations representing 193 member countries, and its annual climate report is one of the highest summaries of global conditions available. The 2023 edition confirmed 2023 as the warmest year on record at 1.45°C above pre-industrial levels. It also documented record ocean heat content, accelerating sea-level rise, and glacier losses at historic scale. Beyond providing data, the tone of the report shaped the emotional direction of the project.
