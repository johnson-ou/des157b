The Air We Share

an interactive storytelling experience on our carbon future

Topic & lenses

The Air We Share is a scroll-driven web experience centered on carbon emissions and climate change. Specifically, how invisible and abstract a crisis can feel when measured in gigatonnes and parts per million. The project draws most heavily from the lenses of data visualization using animated statistics to make abstract data concrete. It also uses speculative design, inviting users to collectively imagine futures they want to exist, and utopian / dystopian narratives, structuring the experience into crisis, followed by a turn toward hope.

Features & libraries

The experience is broken up into a few chapters that unfold as the user scrolls. Early sections use GSAP's ScrollTrigger to animate prose lines and statistics into view. A horizontally-pinned section (inspired directly by the GSAP website) walks through the five major sources of global emissions as card panels. The experience ends in an interactive Three.js 3D forest scene, where users can click anywhere on the ground or trees to leave a wish for the future. Wishes are stored communally via Back4App so every visitor can see what others have left behind, represented as small glowing dots in the scene.

Challenges & uncertainties

The biggest ongoing challenge is definitely figuring out tone. Climate content can easily fall into doom, and doom-heavy narratives make people disengage rather than reflect. The transition from the crisis sections into the forest scene has to be smooth. I am also uncertain whether the wish tree actually achieves what I am hoping for, a genuine sense of collective hope rather than a novelty, but will be fun to test with users.

Target audience

The primary audience is anyone that wants change, but more specifically, people in their 20s. People who know that climate change is real and serious but has quietly normalized the anxiety around it. I want this experience to break through that silence, not by lecturing but by slowing someone down and making them sit with the data at the pace of a scroll, then ending somewhere unexpectedly quiet and communal, since we're all in this together. Leaving a wish should feel like a small act of intention rather than a grand gesture. I also think of this as something I would want to revisit in some years, to see what we were wishing for right now in this moment of time.
